Question

The first robot of the milestone is the i2rt YAM, a 6-axis arm on Damiao motors whose host interface is an impedance frame on CAN at 250 Hz (profile P1). Everything on this page is the YAM through that interface with the model-based mapping of ADR 0011: the question is whether the protocol, the classical set and the learned controller carry over to a position-interface arm with a quarter of the Panda's torque and a 12 N force cap, and whether a host-rate student distilled from scratch through the interface meets the across-the-board criterion against the best classical controller of each family.

Setup

Plant. The Menagerie YAM (MIT licence) with its gripper baked at a fixed opening into one rigid tool body (closed for the surface families and the push, 46 mm for the pick), torque motors on the six joints capped at the motors' peak torques (28 Nm on the first three, 10 Nm on the wrist), the Menagerie armature and 0.1 Nm of dry friction per joint, the model's own gravity compensation switched off (the controllers compensate gravity), and the protocol's environment scaled to the 0.78 m reach: a 6 cm table block and a wall at 0.58 m. The wrist points straight down only close to the base, so the table region is nearer than the Panda's and the blocked and task families tilt the tool 10 to 25 degrees forward.

Protocol v4 scaled to the YAM (ADR 0011, decision 7d): force cap 12 N, press band 5 to 8 N (the press law 5 N + 500 N/m times a commanded depth of 0 to 6 mm), 5 N hold pulses, evaluation payloads of 0, 0.5 and 1 kg at the tool, the heavy-payload group at 1.5 kg, instance deviations as on the Panda. The families, splits, held-out groups and composites are those of protocol v3.

Classical set. The i2rt driver's shipped gains (kp 80/80/80/10/10/10, kd 5/5/5/1.5/1.5/1.5) with and without the inverse-dynamics feedforward of the reference, and the PD re-tuned by CMA-ES through P1 on the cap composite in the feedforward form (40 iterations of 12 candidates on four tune cases per family, the Milestone 9 budget); the best of the three per family is the reference; the oracle around the re-tuned PD is the ceiling.

Student. A fresh gain-residual network (four layers of 256, a history of 25 host frames at 4 ms, the external-torque estimate from the robot model, the feedforward base with the re-tuned gains) distilled by DAgger from two teachers through the interface: the ideal-robot labels in free space and the oracle in contact with the YAM's 5 N floor, twelve iterations of 48 episodes, the teacher driving in the first two, free-space labels weighted three times. It decides at every host frame with no compute latency; the bus delay stays.

Findings

  1. The protocol and the classical set carry over, and the YAM's gains needed re-tuning. Every family, group and task of protocol v4 runs on the YAM through its 250 Hz impedance interface with no divergence. The i2rt driver's shipped gains are a teleoperation setting: 30 mrad on hold, 75 mrad in free motion, 50 mrad on blocked targets, 15 N above the 12 N cap, one grasp in five. The CMA-ES re-tune through the interface (kp 148/61/121/143/52/23 Nm/rad against the shipped 80/80/80/10/10/10) halves the free-motion error (29 mrad), brings hold to 14 mrad, the press into the 5 to 8 N band (8.3 N mean), blocked targets to 9.5 N above the cap, and the grasp to 9 of 16; the feedforward adds nothing to the shipped gains on the families the YAM's small gravity torques dominate. The torque-tracking family through the YAM's own interface puts the realised torque within 0.1 to 1.3% of the motors' peak torque with a 1 to 2 ms delay and a chirp bandwidth of 94 to 125 Hz, as on the Panda's P1.
  2. The host-rate student meets the across-the-board criterion on 32 of 33 rows, in one seed. Distilled from scratch through the interface in twelve iterations (its best validation at the third, 1.78 against 2.41 for its warm start), the student is within 5% of or better than the best classical controller on every family and group but one: hold 1.01 against 2.69 (4 against 14 mrad), free motion at parity (28 against 29 mrad, 1.79 against 1.77), policy-like targets at parity, steps 2.06 against 3.06, press-and-slide 1.08 against 1.29 (pressing at 6.7 N inside the band), blocked targets 3.38 against 5.19 (5.1 against 9.5 N above the cap, contact stiffness 449 against 581 N/m) and 3.61 for the oracle, the task family 1.00 against 1.92 and 1.70 for the oracle. It holds across the held-out groups (free motion on the held-out workspace 1.70 against 2.09, the 1.5 kg payload 3.73 against 4.16 with blocked 5.13 against 8.36, the doubled instance deviations 3.21 against 5.17 on blocked targets, the compliant surfaces 0.69 to 2.63) and on the tasks: 16 of 16 grasps against 9 (placing every block, the family composite 0.71 against 4.18) and 6 of 16 pushes against 4. The one miss is the blocked family on the 500 N/m surface, +22% against the shipped gains, which win that row with a torque rate of 43 Nm/s against the student's 173 while the student presses less (0.2 against 1.3 N above the cap).
  3. Where the student pays. Its torque rate is above the best classical controller's on every family (free motion 392 against 325 Nm/s, policy-like targets 1293 against 998, blocked 148 against 116), the price of the gain multipliers and the residual on a coarse interface; and it beats the oracle only where the oracle's own frame path through the interface softens it (blocked 3.38 against 3.61, the task family 1.00 against 1.70), not on the compliant surfaces (2.39 and 2.63 against 1.46 and 1.45 on blocked targets).

Caveats and failures

  • The YAM plant is datasheet-grounded (motor torque limits, the Menagerie inertias and armature) with no identification data; the DC-motor layer is not modelled, so the servo's loop is the PD law in torque units on the quantised measurement.
  • One seed of the student; the classical gains tuned once; sixteen cases per family.
  • The environment regions and tilts were chosen for reachability, not from any task of the YAM's users.

Next steps

  • The DC-motor layer under the servo law (the DM4310's current loop, friction and backlash from the datasheets) and the Phase 4 transfer check; then the Koch arm on the Dynamixel profile with the identified XM430 constants.
