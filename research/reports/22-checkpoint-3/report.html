Summary

Verdict. The learned low-level controller of Checkpoint 2 carries over to position-controlled arms as a host-rate student: a network that decides once per host frame on the servo's quantised feedback and sends an impedance frame, distilled from scratch through the interface by the two-teacher recipe. On the i2rt YAM through its own interface (MIT mode on CAN at 250 Hz) the student meets the across-the-board criterion on 32 of 33 family-and-group rows against the best classical controller through the same interface, in one seed, and keeps every row under a DC-motor layer whose unknowns are randomised per case. On the Panda, where the same profiles are an ablation of the interface alone, the host-rate students meet 24 to 26 of 33 rows through the three position profiles against the torque-interface classical set (18 to 22 once the PD re-tuned through the profile joins it), winning hold, steps, the task family and the pick and losing the blocked family and the fast free-space families through their torque rate; keeping the teacher in the loop for three more iterations takes the blocked family in two of three seeds (23 of 33 rows pooled, through P1). A learned interface below the controller finds nothing under the model-based one: with the reference advanced over the bus delay and half the hold, the classical mapping is at the floor the servo's own loop sets. The torque rate every controller pays through a position interface is the interface's own, the held setpoint's staircase on a quantisation floor, not an assumption about the servo's velocity estimate that hardware would relax.

What changed since Checkpoint 2. A servo layer with four interface profiles and a model-based mapping; protocol v4 (a torque-tracking family, the pick with a grasp); the plant generalised to a second robot (the YAM) with the protocol's force scale set per robot; host-rate students at the host period with deeper trunks; a probing study with contact-geometry targets; a DC-motor layer under the servo law; and the blocked family at the host rate on the Panda diagnosed to the impact transient under payload, with the recipe varied in label space (section 6).

1. The question

Most arms a laboratory can buy are position-controlled: the host sends a goal, or an impedance frame, over a bus at 50 to 250 Hz, and a servo loop inside the motor holds it. The controller of Checkpoint 2 commands torque at 1 kHz. Milestone 11 asks what of it survives when the torque abstraction has to pass through such an interface, and what a learned controller should be on an arm of that kind: a learned interface that makes the servo behave like a torque source, the controller itself rebuilt at the host rate, or both. The advisor's framing: one controller per robot type, the learned controller as the torque-command interface, hardware not yet allowed, so everything is in simulation with the interface modelled from the drivers and the datasheets, and identified where public data allows.

2. The interface model

The plant takes a servo profile: how often the host may command, with what channels and resolution, how long the bus takes, and what the servo holds between frames. Every controller of the project exposes an impedance frame (goal position and velocity, gains, feedforward torque, cap); a torque command is the frame with zero gains, held with the servo's own spring. The model-based interface (the classical baseline) maps the frame to the profile's channels so that the servo's torque equals the commanded law at the frame's arrival: the reference advanced over the bus delay and half the hold (zero-order-hold compensation), the measured state predicted over the delay, the channels the profile lacks folded into the goal. The servo law runs at 1 kHz on the quantised measurement. On the Panda the servo's loop is abstracted as the tuned PD in torque units, so that the profiles change the interface and nothing else; on the YAM a DC-motor layer with randomised unknowns sits under the law for the robustness check.

3. Protocol v4

Protocol v4 adds to v3 a torque-tracking family (holds, steps, chirps up to the host Nyquist rate, pure-torque chirps on the upper command; metrics: realised-torque error as a fraction of the stall torque, delay, bandwidth, saturation, torque rate) and the pick with a grasp: approach, descend over the block at a fixed opening, grasp (the weld activates only if the block's centre is inside the gap at the grasp instant), lift, carry, place; grasp success, the block's disturbance before the grasp and the place metrics. The force scale is set per robot (Panda cap 15 N, band 7 to 12 N; YAM cap 12 N, band 5 to 8 N); the families, splits, held-out groups and composites are those of v3, and the criterion is ADR 0010's through the interface: within 5% of the best classical controller of each family through the same profile, or better.

4. The classical set through the profiles (Panda)

The same classical controllers through the four profiles. Tracking and contact behaviour survive every profile (the realised contact stiffness within 3% of the torque interface's); smoothness does not (free-motion torque rate 115, 587, 1074 and 1710 Nm/s from P0 to P3). The torque-tracking family separates the profiles the way the bus does (0.4 to 1.3% of the stall torque at P1 with delays of 1 to 2 ms and bandwidth above 100 Hz; 2 to 3% at P2 and P3 with 2 to 10 ms and 10 to 49 Hz). The host period matters beyond 20 ms, the bus delay hardly up to 10 ms. The PD re-tuned through each profile halves its stiffness and trades accuracy for compliance and smoothness; the velocity-feedback filter sweep bounds the raw-velocity assumption at a tenth of the torque rate, and removing the interface's sources one at a time through P1 orders them: the hold and the bus delay first (587 to 263 Nm/s in free motion), the feedback quantisation second (to 186 with both gone, against 115 at the torque interface), the command channels' 12 bits last and only once the others are gone.

5. The learned interface

A network per profile that takes the upper frame and the proprioceptive history at the host rate and emits bounded corrections on the channels the profile has (a goal offset, gain multipliers where writable, a feedforward residual), trained by backpropagation through the plant on the torque-tracking family against the realised torque at every physics step. On no profile does it halve the model-based interface's error (criterion i). The error-floor decomposition explains why: with the reference advanced to the middle of the hold, the model-based interface is at the floor the servo's own loop sets between frames (removing the hold and the delay together leaves 0.5% at P1, from the feedback and command quantisation); a scan of constant corrections finds nothing below it either. The lesson is a modelling one: the first version of the mapping did not advance the reference over the delay, and the learned interface's apparent gains in that version were the model's omission.

6. The host-rate students (Panda)

A fresh gain-residual network per profile (four layers of 256; a history of 100 ms at the host rate; the external-torque estimate; the feedforward PD base), distilled by DAgger through the profile from the ideal-robot labels in free space and the oracle in contact, deciding once per host frame with no compute latency and the bus delay kept. Through P1, P2 and P3 the students meet 25, 24 and 26 of 33 rows against the torque-interface classical set through the same profile, and 21, 22 and 18 once the PD re-tuned through the profile joins it: they win hold (1.3 to 1.5 against 8.2 mrad), steps, the task family and the pick (14 to 16 of 16 grasps against 7), keep free motion within 3 to 7%, and miss the blocked family and the fast free-space families through their torque rate. No training or evaluation episode diverged through any profile (an earlier reading to the contrary was an artefact of a flag, corrected in report 21's erratum).

7. The YAM

The Menagerie YAM with its gripper baked into the tool body, torque motors at the DM motors' peak torques, the environment scaled to its reach, protocol v4 at its force scale. The shipped driver gains are a teleoperation setting (30 mrad on hold, 15 N above the cap); the CMA-ES re-tune through the interface halves the free-motion error and brings the press into the band. The host-rate student distilled from scratch through P1 (four layers of 256, one seed, selected at its third iteration) meets the criterion on 32 of 33 rows: hold 4 against 14 mrad, free motion at parity, blocked 5.1 against 9.5 N above the 12 N cap, 16 of 16 grasps against 9; the miss is blocked on the 500 N/m surface through its torque rate. Under the randomised DC-motor layer (torque-constant error ±15%, voltage envelope, friction, current-loop lag up to 2 ms, 12-bit torque feedback) it keeps every one of the 16 evaluated rows, and the layer's lag smooths every controller by about 40%.

8. What the representations encode

Linear probes from the observation and from every trunk layer, replayed through the profile on validation episodes, with a nonlinear probe beside the linear one. The deeper host-rate trunks decode contact at 0.84 to 0.94 balanced accuracy (the two-layer torque-interface student 0.77 to 0.80), the reference's depth inside the surface at R² 0.75 to 0.90 (the quantity the impedance law needs), sliding against pressing at 0.77 to 0.82, and the blocked and release phases at 0.85 to 0.97; the surface normal in the tool frame is readable from the observation and, on the YAM, from the layers (R² 0.42 to 0.66, 0.80 nonlinearly); the stiffness classes, the payload and the contact force are not linearly decodable from any layer. The block-in-the-gap probe before the grasp reads 0.88 to 0.97, but so does the observation (0.90): it reads the plan's phase, not the block.

9. Corrections made during the milestone

  • The model-based interface did not advance the reference over the bus delay and the hold. Found by the error-floor decomposition; fixed to the middle of the hold (zero-order-hold compensation); every Phase 1 and 2 table re-run; the pre-fix results kept under runs/m11/pre_fix and not reported.
  • The torque-tracking metric's command ignored the reference's motion within a hold. Fixed: the command is the latest frame's law with the current reference at the true state, so the hold and the delay both count as error.
  • The oracle's frame path indexed its reference by call count. Through a profile it ran four times too slow; fixed to time indexing and the oracle rows re-evaluated.
  • Report 21's collection divergences were a flag's artefact. The free-space labelling path compared the labelled decisions with a tenth of the physics steps; at 50 Hz every episode tripped it. No episode diverged; the finding and its mechanism were withdrawn in an erratum, the flag now counts decisions.

10. Lessons

  • Model the interface exactly before learning it. The learned interface's only apparent gain was the model's omission of the delay compensation; once the classical mapping was exact at the frame's arrival it sat at the servo's floor and the network found nothing below it. A learned interface is worth revisiting only on a servo whose loop the model gets wrong (friction, backlash, a firmware filter), which is what the DC-motor layer and hardware measurements are for.
  • Rebuild the controller at the host rate rather than wrap the 1 kHz one. The host-rate student, distilled from scratch through the profile with its history at the host rate, recovered the torque-interface controller's wins on hold, steps, tasks and the pick through every profile; deeper trunks encode contact sharper than the two-layer student.
  • The classical set must include the PD tuned through the profile. Softening is the classical answer to a position interface; it halves the blocked excess and the torque rate at the price of accuracy. The criterion's classical reference through a profile includes it.
  • A one-sided metric around a target at the cap punishes variance. The student's push error along the normal is unbiased, yet the excess above the cap grows with its spread, most under payload at the impact; a label term in force space addresses what the torque loss cannot see.
  • The torque rate through a position interface is structural. The held setpoint moves by the reference velocity times the period at every frame; neither a filtered velocity estimate nor a softer PD recovers the torque interface's smoothness. Only a firmware that interpolates the goal between frames would, and that is a measurement for later.
  • Check flags against the rate they were written for. A divergence threshold in physics steps read every 50 Hz episode as diverged and produced a confident mechanism for a non-event.

11. Next

  • The sustained-contact tasks proposed in the context document (wiping under a force band, drawer and door, chamfered peg insertion, an object along a wall, a lid, erasing with a held tool) are the task-level signal the milestone did not have.
  • Inference time: the simulation paused for the network this round; the host-rate students' four layers of 256 at 250 Hz are the budget to meet, by distillation into a smaller network or by measurement on the host.
  • The Dynamixel arm (Koch) on P2 with the XM430 constants identified on the public dataset, and with it the transfer check to a data-fitted actuator surrogate; deferred by the advisor's decision.
  • Hardware measurements when allowed: the bus period and delay, the velocity estimate the firmware damps on, and whether the goal is interpolated between frames.
  • A payload estimate as an explicit input (or a label term that makes the student infer it) for the blocked family under payload, where the oracle's privilege is the payload's weight along the normal.
