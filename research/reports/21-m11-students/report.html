Question

Phase 2b asks whether the learned controller of reports 15 to 18 can be rebuilt natively on a position interface: a student that decides once per host frame on the quantised feedback, whose fast law is the servo's own loop, distilled from scratch through the profile by the two-teacher recipe, with a deeper trunk (four layers of 256) and no compute latency, as the advisor allowed for this round. The criterion is the one of ADR 0010 through the interface: within 5% of the best classical controller of each family through the same profile, or better, with the oracle through the profile as the ceiling.

Setup

Students. One per profile (P1 at 250 Hz, P2 at 100 Hz, P3 at 50 Hz), gain-residual form on the feedforward PD base with the tracking-tuned gains, the external-torque estimate in the history, a history of 100 ms at the host rate (25, 10 and 5 frames), four layers of 256; distilled by DAgger from the ideal-robot labels in free space and the oracle around the feedforward PD in contact (7 N floor), twelve iterations of 48 episodes, the teacher driving in the first two, free-space labels weighted three times; every episode runs through the profile's servo loop with the model-based interface, the student's frame mapped like any other.

Benchmark. Protocol v4 through the profile: every group of protocol v3, the task families with the grasp, and the torque-tracking family; the classical set and the oracle through the same profile; the student's trunk probed layer by layer as in report 19.

Findings

  1. Through every profile the host-rate student rebuilds most of the Panda controller and misses the blocked family. Distilled from scratch (best validation at the eleventh, eleventh and ninth iteration through P1, P2 and P3; 3.45, 3.73 and 4.04 against 4.21, 4.42 and 4.58 for the feedforward PD through the same profile), the students meet 25, 24 and 26 of 33 rows with no divergence in any group. They win hold on every profile (1.3 to 1.5 against 8.2 mrad), steps (2.5 to 2.7 against 3.3 to 3.6), the task family (3.4 to 3.6 against 4.4 to 4.8, better than the oracle through P1 and P3), press-and-slide on P1 and P3, and the pick: 16, 16 and 14 of 16 grasps against 7, with the place force unchanged. Free motion is within 3% on P1 and P3 and 7% on P2 (2.1 to 4.3 against 5.0 to 6.4 mrad, at a higher torque rate), the policy-like targets within 4 to 8%. The misses are the blocked family in every group but one (11.7, 8.8 and 11.5 against 10.6 in distribution, where the oracle through the profile is at 7.4, 5.2 and 5.5; 84, 36 and 49 N above the cap with the 3 kg payload against 43), free motion and policy-like targets at the two speeds through the torque rate (P2 +17% to +33%, P3 +7% to +11%), and the push family on P2 and P3 (+23% and +17%, 7 and 3 successes against 5 and 4). Every student presses above the 7 to 12 N band on the sliding families (17 to 23 N; the feedforward PD 18): the contact impedance did not distil through the interface in twelve iterations as it did at 1 kHz on the Panda, while the ideal-robot labels in free space did. The YAM student of report 20 took the blocked family with the same recipe, so the Panda's stiffer gains and its 15 N cap against the YAM's 12 N on a quarter of the torque are the likely difference.
  2. Collection through the goal-position profiles diverges; evaluation does not. During DAgger the student-driven training episodes diverged in 5 to 9 of 48 per iteration through P2 and in 23 of 48 at the second iteration through P3, whereas no evaluation case diverged for any student. The training cases carry payloads to 3 kg and fresh instances; through P2 and P3 the student's gain multipliers above the servo's stiffness are folded into the goal offset and held for 10 or 20 ms, which is open-loop stiffness. The best checkpoints are selected on validation and do not show it, but the label store of those profiles holds fewer decisions (248 000 and 33 000 rows against 300 000 for P1).
  3. Depth and the host rate buy a sharper contact representation. Probed layer by layer through their profiles, the four-layer students decode contact at 0.84 to 0.87 (P1), 0.89 to 0.94 (P2) and 0.85 to 0.90 (P3) balanced accuracy, detecting 54 to 88% of onsets within 50 ms, against 0.77 to 0.80 and 30 to 44% for the two-layer Panda student of report 19; the surface normal is readable from their observations (R² 0.68 to 0.76) and from no layer, the stiffness classes stay at chance, the payload and the contact force are not linearly decodable.

Caveats and failures

  • One seed per profile; the students are distilled from scratch, without the actor-critic base of the Panda students, so their free-space part rests on the ideal-robot labels alone.
  • The oracle and the classical set are through the same profile, so the comparison is fair to the interface but every ceiling is itself interface-limited.
