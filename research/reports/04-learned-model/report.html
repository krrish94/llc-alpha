Question

Can a dynamics model learned from plant data replace the known rigid-body model as the training medium? Three separate claims are tested: (1) prediction accuracy at several horizons and regimes, (2) usefulness of the model's gradients, measured against the known model, and (3) closed-loop performance in the plant of controllers trained through the learned model, compared with the same parameterizations trained through the known model (Milestone 2) and with the conventional baselines.

Setup

Data: closed-loop episodes in the plant under PD control with shared gains scaled by a random factor (0.3 to 1.5), filtered exploration torque noise (0 to 10% of the torque limits) and references of all six regimes at random speed scales (24 training and 6 validation episodes per regime, 1 kHz). Two model classes at the 2.5 ms model step: a black-box network predicting the joint acceleration and the contact force magnitude from (q, qd, tau), and a residual network added to the known free-space rigid-body model. Both are trained for 15000 steps on 270k transitions. Controllers are then trained through the frozen residual model (all three parameterizations) and through the black-box model (learned gains) with the protocol of ADR 0003 and evaluated in the plant on the test split.

Model validation

Open-loop prediction error is measured by replaying recorded torques from 20 start points per validation episode. Gradient agreement compares the sensitivity of the joint velocity 100 ms ahead to a held torque perturbation between the learned model and the known rigid-body model at free-space points (where the known model is exact).

Findings

  1. Prediction: both learned models are accurate at the control horizon and drift open loop over a second. After one 2.5 ms step the position error is below 0.3 mrad in every regime; after 100 ms it is 2 to 14 mrad (largest for step targets, smallest in contact where the tool is held by the surface); after 1 s of open-loop replay it reaches 0.03 to 0.6 rad (black-box) and up to 1.1 rad for transitions (residual). The plant is open-loop unstable under gravity, so the 1 s figure measures drift accumulation, not usefulness for closed-loop training, where the loop corrects the state every 10 ms.
  2. Gradients agree with the known model. The sensitivity of the joint velocity 100 ms ahead to a torque perturbation has a mean cosine similarity of 0.96 (black-box) and 0.97 (residual) with the known model's, with minimum 0.62 and 0.81 over 64 free-space points, and a relative norm error of 0.37 and 0.34. The residual model, which starts from the exact free-space dynamics, has the more reliable gradients.
  3. Closed loop: training through the learned model gives the same controllers as training through the known model. Mean composites over regimes: learned gains 1.56 (residual model) and 1.50 (black-box) against 1.58 (known model); PD + residual 1.37 against 1.35; neural torque 1.86 against 1.75; PD with shared gains 1.37. Per metric the routes agree closely (for example smooth-tracking position RMSE 1.7 mrad for PD + residual on either route; policy-like-target torque rate 1787 against 1291 Nm/s for learned gains). The learned model is therefore an adequate substitute for the known model as a training medium under this protocol, and it inherits the protocol v1 shortfall of Milestone 2 exactly.
  4. Cost: the black-box model is three times cheaper to train through (6 min for 320 iterations against 21 min for the residual model and 19 to 25 min for the rigid-body model), because one network evaluation replaces the recursive Newton-Euler pass, and it produced the best learned-gains controller of the three routes (composite 1.50; hold pulse deflection 7.1 mm against 9.3 mm for the known route).
  5. Contact remains the weak point of every model. Learned gains trained through the residual model lose contact 7 times per sustained-contact case in the plant (4 for the known route, 0 for PD); neural torque trained through the learned model chatters less than through the known model (4.6 against 23.8 losses) but tracks worse. None of the training media reproduce the plant's stick-slip.

Caveats and failures

  • One seed per route and parameterization; the differences between routes (for example 1.50 against 1.58 for learned gains) are within what a seed could change and are not claimed as effects.
  • The data were collected under PD control with exploration noise; the coverage audit of states visited by the trained controllers was not run in this pass. The agreement between routes is indirect evidence that coverage was adequate.
  • The learned models predict the contact force magnitude only, so the force-rate and force terms of the training loss act on a scalar; the tool-contact direction is not modelled.
  • All results are under training protocol v1 (no disturbance pulses in training), so the absolute performance carries the Milestone 2 diagnosis; the comparison between routes is unaffected because both routes used the same protocol.

Next steps

Repeat the learned-model route under protocol v2 once the Milestone 2 v2 runs confirm the protocol, add seeds, and run the coverage audit (distance of controller-visited states to the training data and model error there). A second data-aggregation round under the trained controllers is prepared but was not run.
